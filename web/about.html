About Open Data Discover

The City of Toronto's Open Data Portal contains hundreds of public datasets—tracking everything from neighborhood park locations and to transit schedules. However, figuring out how different datasets can be connected or combined can be challenging.

Open Data Discover aims to improve the discoverability of Toronto's open datasets, by making it easier to see the structure of each dataset's resources and showing possible joins across different sets.

Whether you are already using a dataset and want an easy way to see other datasets you can combine or incorporate into your project, or are just getting started on a project and want to explore what public data the city has to offer, this tool is designed to make Toronto's open data easier to explore and use.

The Data

Open Data Toronto is the official open data portal maintained by the City of Toronto. Its core purpose is to make municipal government data freely available to the public in open, machine-readable formats.

Goal: Promotes civic transparency, data-driven decision-making, municipal accountability, and public innovation. It allows community members, developers, academics, journalists, and businesses to build apps, analyze urban trends, or conduct research.

Licensing: All datasets are released under the Open Government Licence – Toronto. This licence allows worldwide, royalty-free, perpetual use, modification, and redistribution for both commercial and non-commercial purposes, provided appropriate attribution is given to the City.

Available Datasets & Formats

The catalog contains hundreds of datasets spanning dozens of City divisions and agencies. Key categories include:

  • Transportation & Mobility: Road closures, traffic signals, cycling routes, TTC transit schedules, parking locations, and collision statistics.
  • Public Health & Safety: Shelter occupancy and capacity, DineSafe restaurant inspection records, paramedic response times, and Toronto Police Service event logs.
  • Parks, Forestry & Recreation: Urban tree canopy inventories, park facility locations, trail networks, and recreational program listings.
  • City Governance & Finance: Approved municipal budgets, lobbyist registry data, council votes, active business licenses, and short-term rental registrations.
  • Environment & Infrastructure: Garbage/recycling collection schedules, water meter data, green roof locations, and energy consumption.
  • Geospatial & Mapping: City ward boundaries, property parcels, elevation data, and 3D massing models.

Formats: Data can typically be downloaded in standard structured formats such as CSV, GeoJSON, SHP (Shapefile), JSON, and XML, or queried directly via the portal's open API (CKAN API).

How Current Is the Data?

Data freshness varies depending on the specific dataset and its underlying operational source.

Refresh Rates

Each dataset on the portal lists an official "Refresh Rate" metadata field set by the publishing division:

  • Daily / Real-Time: Frequently updated feeds (e.g., daily shelter occupancy, active traffic events, dynamic transit schedules).
  • As Available: Updated dynamically as new events or administrative changes occur.
  • Monthly / Quarterly: Operational records updated on fixed cycles (e.g., DineSafe inspection logs, housing statistics).
  • Annually: Year-end or cycle-based datasets (e.g., annual municipal budgets, major financial reports).
  • Will Not Be Refreshed / Historical: One-off studies, legacy datasets, or archived historical collections.

Timestamp

Every dataset page includes a "Data Last Refreshed" timestamp. This field shows the date and time the portal's automated pipeline last pulled data from the source department.

The Method

Candidate Joins & Profiling

A candidate join represents a pair of columns from two different CSV files in the Open Data catalog that share overlapping values. To discover these connections, the analysis pipeline profiles every complete dataset in the catalog, building unique value lists for each column after standardizing text (converting to lowercase and removing extra spaces).

Containment & Score Calculation

To determine how strongly two columns relate, the algorithm evaluates set overlap based on shared unique values:

  • Shared Unique Values: The total number of distinct entries that appear in both Column A and Column B.
  • Containment Ratio: Measures what percentage of Column A's unique entries can be found inside Column B:
    Containment (A to B) = Shared Unique Values ÷ Distinct Values in Column A
  • Overall Score: Takes the higher of the two directional containment ratios:
    Overall Score = Maximum of (Containment A to B, Containment B to A)

Using the maximum directional containment score (ranging from 0.00 to 1.00) ensures that subset relationships stand out—such as a list of 20 neighborhood parks whose names are entirely contained within a larger city-wide inventory of 1,000 parks.

Algorithmic Scope & Limitations

To maintain high performance across hundreds of datasets, the detection pipeline operates within defined technical boundaries:

  • Exact Text Matching: Comparisons require exact text matches. The algorithm does not use NLP or fuzzy matching, so variations like "Avenue" versus "Ave" are not counted as matching values.
  • Skipped Short & System Columns: System keys (such as _id), columns with fewer than 8 unique entries, or columns averaging 6 characters or fewer per cell are excluded to filter out generic identifiers, dates, and status codes.
  • Common Value Filtering: Extremely frequent terms that appear across dozens of different datasets are excluded to prevent cluttering results with trivial matches.
  • Coincidental Overlaps: High scores reflect statistical string overlap rather than guaranteed meaning. Unrelated datasets sharing identical code numbers or common names may occasionally yield high scores by chance.

How to Use This Tool

Select a Dataset

Search or use the drop-down in the Data section to select a dataset. Then select a column to view its join results.

View Results

The score next to each result shows how strongly its values overlap with your selected column—a higher score means a higher likelihood that the two datasets can be connected or combined.

Clicking any result row opens that dataset and displays its matching columns, allowing you to follow connections and explore related data across the entire catalog.
